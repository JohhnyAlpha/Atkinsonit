Why SMEs Must Know What's Hosted Where

22 June 2026

Home › Blog › Why SMEs Must Know What's Hosted Where

This week’s work: unravelling a hosting puzzle

I’ve spent a fair amount of time this week working through a client’s hosting setup — something that started as a simple question but quickly turned into a full discovery exercise. Like many SMEs, they weren’t aware of how many different providers were involved, or how each layer depends on the others. It’s more complex than it looks from the outside, and it’s a perfect reminder of why every business needs a clear record of what’s hosted where, and who holds the keys.

The invisible web of providers

Your online presence is rarely in one place. A typical SME setup looks like this:

Function Common Provider What It Does
Domain Registrar IONOS, GoDaddy Owns your domain name
DNS Hosting Cloudflare, AWS Route 53 Directs traffic to the right servers
Website Hosting Squarespace, Wix, WordPress Stores and serves your website
Email Hosting Google Workspace, Microsoft 365 Manages your business email

Why credentials matter

If you don’t know who holds the credentials, you don’t control your online presence.

  • You can’t renew your domain (and risk losing it).
  • You can’t fix DNS issues that take your site offline.
  • You can’t update SPF, DKIM or DMARC for email security.
  • You can’t migrate or secure your website properly.

Why “unpicking” one layer can break the others

These layers are connected. Change one without understanding the rest, and you can easily break something critical.

  • Changing DNS without checking email: A DNS update overwrites MX, SPF or DKIM — email stops working.
  • Moving the website but not updating DNS: Visitors still see the old site, or a 404 page.
  • Transferring the domain registrar: If nameservers aren’t copied correctly, both website and email can go dark.
  • Letting a third party “tidy things up”: They remove records that other services quietly depend on.

The fix: a simple digital asset register

Every SME should maintain a basic record of their digital assets. It doesn’t need to be complicated — even a spreadsheet works.

Item What to Record
Domain Registrar, renewal date, login owner
DNS Provider, nameservers, who manages changes
Website Hosting Platform, billing owner, admin access
Email Hosting Provider, MX/SPF/DKIM settings, admin account
Other Services Integrations, API keys, third‑party tools

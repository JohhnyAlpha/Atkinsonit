How We Modernised a Legacy Microsoft 365 Tenant in Four Days

20 July 2026

Home › Blog › How We Modernised a Legacy Microsoft 365 Tenant in Four Days

A common SME challenge

Many small businesses run Microsoft 365 tenants that have grown organically over time. Old domains linger. Login behaviour becomes inconsistent. Security falls behind modern standards. Devices drift out of alignment. This project was exactly that scenario — a tenant that worked, but not smoothly.

What we found

The wrong domain was still in charge

The tenant still used a legacy domain as its default. This caused branding drift, DKIM misalignment, and login inconsistency. Users saw different domains in different places — email addresses, login prompts, and external communication — which made the business look less cohesive and introduced subtle trust issues with customers and partners.

Duplicate user identities

The main user had multiple accounts across old domains. Over time, this had created a tangle of identities: different usernames, overlapping mailboxes, and historic logins that still existed in Azure AD. We consolidated everything into one clean identity, simplifying authentication, licensing, and day‑to‑day access while reducing the risk of misdirected email or misconfigured permissions.

Security behind modern standards

MFA wasn’t enabled. Conditional Access wasn’t configured. Legacy protocols were still active. In practice, this meant the tenant relied on passwords alone, allowed older mail clients to connect, and had no structured way to enforce location, device, or risk‑based controls. For a modern Microsoft 365 environment, that’s a significant gap — especially when the same tenant is used for finance, HR, and customer data.

DNS hosted on a legacy platform

DNS was still hosted on a legacy platform that lacked modern tooling, observability, and security features. Changes were slow, visibility was limited, and there was no easy way to introduce protections such as DNSSEC or modern caching behaviour. We migrated DNS to Cloudflare for stability, speed, and security, giving the business a more resilient foundation for email, web, and future cloud services.

Our four‑day modernisation plan

Day 1 — DNS & identity cleanup

On day one, we moved DNS to Cloudflare, set the correct domain as default, and cleaned up identities. That meant updating MX, SPF, and other records to point to the right services, retiring unused domains, and ensuring the primary business identity was consistently represented across Microsoft 365. We also mapped existing accounts, removed duplicates, and aligned usernames and sign‑in addresses with the chosen domain.

Day 2 — DKIM & email trust

Day two focused on email trust. We regenerated DKIM and aligned all email authentication with the business identity. By ensuring SPF, DKIM, and DMARC were correctly configured, we improved deliverability and reduced the risk of spoofing. Outbound email now presents a consistent, authenticated identity, which is critical for invoices, proposals, and customer communication.

Day 3 — Security modernisation

On day three, we modernised security. We enabled MFA, created Conditional Access policies, and blocked legacy protocols. The tenant now enforces strong authentication, restricts access based on risk and context, and prevents older, less secure clients from connecting. This brings the environment in line with current Microsoft 365 security guidance and significantly reduces the attack surface.

Day 4 — Device alignment

The final day was about devices. We fixed Authenticator, cleaned Outlook profiles, registered devices in Azure AD, and removed legacy artefacts. Users now have predictable sign‑in behaviour, Outlook is aligned with the new identity structure, and devices are properly associated with the tenant. This makes future management — from compliance to remote wipe — far more straightforward.

The outcome

At the end of the four days, the business had a clean identity structure, modern security, Cloudflare DNS, reliable email flow, updated devices, and no legacy artefacts. In practical terms, that means fewer surprises, smoother day‑to‑day work, and a tenant designed to last decades rather than just surviving the next upgrade.

A modern Microsoft 365 environment doesn’t just improve security — it makes daily work smoother, more predictable, and more professional. Staff log in the same way everywhere. Email behaves consistently. Devices align with policy. And when the business grows, the tenant is ready rather than needing another round of emergency fixes.

Why this matters for SMEs

For SMEs, Microsoft 365 is often the backbone of communication and collaboration. When tenants grow organically, small misconfigurations accumulate: old domains, half‑finished security settings, devices that were “just added quickly”. None of these issues are dramatic on their own, but together they create friction, risk, and a sense that IT is always slightly out of step with the business.

Taking a structured, time‑boxed approach — like this four‑day modernisation — turns that drift into a solvable engineering problem. By treating identity, DNS, email trust, security, and devices as one coherent system, you get a tenant that supports growth instead of holding it back.

Practical next steps for your tenant

If your Microsoft 365 tenant feels “messy” or has grown over several years, it’s worth asking a few simple questions:

  • Identity clarity: Is there a single, consistent primary domain for logins and email?
  • DNS posture: Is DNS on a modern, observable platform such as Cloudflare?
  • Email trust: Are SPF, DKIM, and DMARC correctly aligned with your business identity?
  • Security baseline: Is MFA enforced and Conditional Access in place for key roles?
  • Device alignment: Are laptops, desktops, and mobiles properly registered and using modern clients?

If the answer to several of these is “not yet”, a focused modernisation project can make a noticeable difference in just a few days. The goal isn’t perfection — it’s a tenant that is clean, secure, and ready for the next decade of business.

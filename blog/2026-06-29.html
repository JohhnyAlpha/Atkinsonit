Why SMEs Keep Getting “AI SEO” Cold Emails — And How to Spot the Red Flags

29 June 2026

If you run a small business, you’ve almost certainly received one of these:

“I specialise in AI-driven mobile apps and SE0 techniques to increase visibility. Can we discuss how I can help your business?”

Sometimes the spelling of SEO even includes a zero. Always a promising start.

These emails arrive weekly for many SMEs — sometimes daily — and they all follow the same pattern: vague promises, AI buzzwords, and a phone number from the other side of the world. So why do these messages exist, and how do you tell what’s legitimate from what’s… less so?

Why These Emails Exist

There are three main reasons SMEs get bombarded with these outreach messages:

  • Mass scraping of business directories. Your email is publicly listed on Google Business, Companies House, or your website. Automated tools harvest it.
  • Cheap, high-volume outreach. Agencies send tens of thousands of emails per day. If even 0.1% reply, it’s profitable.
  • “AI” is the new magic word. Five years ago it was “mobile apps”. Before that, “digital transformation”. Now it’s “AI SEO”.

These emails aren’t personalised. They’re not based on your website. They’re not based on your industry. They’re sent to everyone — from accountants to dog groomers to funeral directors.

Red Flags to Watch Out For

Here are the most common warning signs that an email is generic outreach rather than a genuine, relevant offer:

  • No company name or website. If they can’t show who they are, that’s a problem.
  • Generic claims. “We improve your visibility”, “We use AI”, “We build apps”. No specifics.
  • Spelling errors. “SE0” with a zero is a classic.
  • International phone numbers with no context. +91, +971, +234 — nothing wrong with them, but legitimate providers introduce themselves properly.
  • No reference to your business. Not your name, not your website, not your industry.

These aren’t signs of a scam — just signs of low-quality, high-volume outreach.

What Real SEO and AI Visibility Work Looks Like

Legitimate SEO and AI visibility work is measurable, technical, and specific. It includes things like:

  • Technical audits — site structure, speed, indexing, schema.
  • Entity optimisation — making sure search engines understand who you are and what you do.
  • AI visibility — ensuring your content is structured so AI systems can use it.
  • Local authority building — citations, reviews, trust signals.
  • Content strategy — not “blogs for keywords”, but content that answers real customer questions.

None of this fits into a £99/month “AI SEO package”.

How SMEs Can Protect Themselves

You don’t need to ignore every cold email — but you should evaluate them properly. Here’s how:

  • Ask for examples of their work. Real providers have real case studies.
  • Check their domain age. A website registered last week is a red flag.
  • Look for specificity. If they can’t explain what they’d do for your business, walk away.
  • Avoid long-term contracts. Good SEO doesn’t require locking you in for 12 months.
  • Trust your instincts. If it feels generic, it probably is.

The Bottom Line

Cold outreach isn’t inherently bad — but most of the AI/SEO emails SMEs receive are generic, low-value, and not aligned with your actual needs. Real digital improvement comes from understanding your business, your customers, and your goals.

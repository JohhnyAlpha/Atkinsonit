The VMware Price Shock: When Corporate Strategy Ignores Real‑World Value

4 July 2026

Home › Blog › The VMware Price Shock: When Corporate Strategy Ignores Real‑World Value

From Stable Platform to Pricing Shock

The VMware pricing overhaul has landed, and the reaction across the industry is somewhere between disbelief and outright frustration. A 300–500% cost increase isn’t a rounding error — it’s a fundamental shift in how Broadcom wants VMware to exist in the market. For many organisations, this isn’t a minor adjustment; it’s a forced rethink of whether VMware still belongs in their infrastructure at all.

What Changed: Bundles, Minimums, and Subscriptions

Broadcom hasn’t just nudged prices up. They’ve changed the entire licensing model, collapsed products into bundles nobody asked for, enforced minimum core counts that don’t reflect real‑world deployments, and removed perpetual licences entirely. The result is simple: organisations are being pushed into paying for products they don’t use, at scales they don’t need, on terms they didn’t choose.

Why This Hurts SMEs and Mid‑Market IT

On paper, this makes sense in a boardroom. Fewer customers, bigger contracts, predictable subscription revenue. In practice, it breaks the environments where VMware actually lives — small clusters, mid‑market estates, branch offices, labs, DR sites, and the thousands of businesses that chose VMware because it was flexible, reliable, and scalable without being financially punitive. Those are the estates now seeing 300–500% cost increases for doing exactly what they were doing last year.

VMware’s Real Value — and What Broadcom Missed

VMware’s value was never just vSphere. It was the ecosystem, the community, the partner network, and the ability to scale from two hosts to two thousand without having to rebuild your entire approach to virtualisation. It worked for SMBs, mid‑market, and enterprise, and it did so with a licensing model that roughly matched how people actually deployed it.

Broadcom’s new model doesn’t just ignore that value — it actively dismantles it. By treating virtualisation as a high‑margin, enterprise‑only product, they’ve turned what was once a general‑purpose platform into something closer to a premium cloud stack. That may suit very large estates. It does not suit everyone else.

Is VMware Still the Right Fit?

For many organisations, the question is no longer “can we afford VMware?” but “does VMware still make sense for us at all?” That’s a strategic conversation every IT team should be having now, not when renewal dates are weeks away. What workloads are truly dependent on VMware? Where are you paying for features you don’t use? Which parts of your estate could move to alternative platforms without disrupting the business?

The next 12–18 months will reshape the virtualisation landscape. Some organisations will stay with VMware and absorb the new pricing. Many won’t. But everyone will be forced to make a decision — and that’s the real impact of Broadcom’s strategy.

Practical Next Steps for IT Teams

This isn’t a moment for panic, but it is a moment for clarity. Start by mapping where VMware sits in your environment, what it’s actually doing, and what you’re now being asked to pay for. From there, you can compare options: staying with VMware under the new model, reducing footprint, or planning a phased migration to alternative platforms such as Hyper‑V, Proxmox, Nutanix, or cloud‑native services where appropriate.

The key is to treat this as an engineering decision, not an emotional one. VMware is still a strong technology platform. The question is whether Broadcom’s licensing strategy still aligns with your scale, budget, and roadmap.

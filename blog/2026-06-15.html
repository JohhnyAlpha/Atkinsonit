Weekly SME IT Update

15 June 2026

Microsoft tightens security defaults again

Microsoft has expanded its secure‑by‑default rollout, enforcing stricter Conditional Access baselines across more SME tenants. Legacy authentication is being blocked more aggressively, and older devices may start prompting for re‑authentication more often. If staff suddenly “can’t sign in”, it’s usually this change — not a system fault.

Google Workspace licence and storage reviews recommended

Google has made further adjustments to Workspace pricing, particularly around mixed‑tier environments. Many SMEs are still paying for unused storage or licences that no longer match how people actually work. A short licence and storage audit often frees up budget without disrupting users.

UK phishing activity remains high

The HMRC and Companies House impersonation campaign continues, with attackers using realistic templates and referencing genuine filing dates. SMEs should ensure staff understand that:
• HMRC will not ask for sensitive information via email
• MFA is essential on all critical accounts
• Suspicious messages should be reported, not ignored or deleted quietly

Apple pushes more urgent patches

Apple has released another round of security updates for iOS, iPadOS and macOS, addressing actively exploited vulnerabilities. SMEs relying on Apple devices should confirm automatic updates are enabled and that older devices are still receiving security patches. Manual or ad‑hoc patching leaves gaps that attackers increasingly know how to find.

Cyber insurers increasing evidence requirements

Cyber insurers are now asking for proof — not just policy statements — that MFA, patching, email filtering and privileged access controls are in place. Businesses unable to demonstrate these controls are seeing higher premiums, more questions at renewal, or delays in cover. Keeping simple evidence (screenshots, reports, policy documents) makes renewals much smoother.

Baldur’s Gate 3 on macOS — a small but relevant note

A number of macOS users have reported issues launching or updating Baldur’s Gate 3, linked to a mix of sandboxing restrictions and how the game handles large asset updates. While this is “just a game”, it highlights a broader SME reality: macOS can still be tripped up by applications that don’t fully align with Apple’s security and packaging model. If your business relies on Mac software outside the App Store — creative tools, engineering apps, or legacy utilities — similar behaviours can occur. Ensuring devices are fully patched, standardising how software is installed, and avoiding a mix of App Store and direct‑download versions of the same tool all help reduce these issues.

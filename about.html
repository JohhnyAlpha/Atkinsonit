About the Founder — Bernard Atkinson

Atkinson IT is led by Bernard Atkinson, a heritage‑grade systems engineer whose career spans...

Atkinson IT is led by Bernard Atkinson, a heritage‑grade systems engineer whose career spans nearly four decades of hands‑on computing — from 8‑bit home machines to global enterprise infrastructure. Beginning in 1985, Bernard entered IT at a time when systems demanded real engineering, deep understanding, and the ability to work directly with hardware, operating systems, and proprietary architectures.

Bernard’s foundations began with the Commodore 64 and Amiga, learning computing at the metal — timing, memory, registers, and optimisation. This early discipline shaped a career defined by precision, clarity, and deep systems understanding.

Prime Computers — Engineering from First Principles

Bernard’s first professional break came as a YTS engineer at Prime Computers in 1987, supporting PRIMOS, minicomputer architectures, and hybrid PC/greenscreen estates. This period forged Bernard’s systems‑thinking mindset and his ability to diagnose issues from first principles — a skill that continues to define Atkinson IT today.

Enterprise Infrastructure — Citibank, LDDC, Diners Club

In 1989, Bernard joined Citibank, supporting early enterprise PC deployments, DOS/Windows hybrids, and the transition from standalone systems to networked environments. He later joined the London Docklands Development Corporation (LDDC), supporting a diverse estate of DOS, early Windows, Prime minis, and line‑of‑business systems during one of London’s most significant redevelopment phases.

From 1995 to 2000, Bernard worked for Diners Club International across Farnborough and Zurich, supporting mission‑critical financial platforms, cross‑border infrastructure, and the integration of legacy minis, early Windows systems, and bespoke financial modelling tools.

Global Engineering — WPP

Bernard’s enterprise career continued at WPP, where he played a key role in global infrastructure consolidation across 40+ companies and 4,500+ users. His work included stabilising and modernising financial modelling systems, supporting M&A integration, and delivering resilient storage, virtualisation, and networking platforms.

A Living Timeline of Computing

Across his career, Bernard has engineered solutions across:

  • Windows 3.x, NT, 2000, XP SP2, and modern Windows
  • Novell NetWare and early directory services
  • AS/400, greenscreen systems, and midrange architectures
  • Legacy POS and finance systems including Hyperion and SunSystems
  • Enterprise networking, domains, and authentication models
  • Virtualisation, storage, and modern SME infrastructure

This breadth gives Bernard a unique capability: understanding systems from first principles, stabilising environments others consider “legacy”, and modernising infrastructure without breaking what still works.

This combination of heritage knowledge and modern engineering allows Atkinson IT to stabilise legacy systems, build resilient new platforms, and integrate both into a single coherent environment.
